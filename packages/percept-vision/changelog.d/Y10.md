### Delivery — provisional incident + superseding revision

A correction that lands 21-34s after the original claim is no longer either lost or bought at the cost
of latency for every claim. Percept now emits the finding **provisionally** on the rising edge and, when
the mutation channel classifies a later turn as replace/retract on the **same** episode
(`EpisodeFoldResult.supersedes`), emits a **superseding revision** linked back to the incident it
corrects — so a consumer sees BOTH the original and the correction with the relation between them,
instead of a coequal unlinked peer. It does **not** withhold every incident for a settle window wide
enough to catch a 34s retraction.

- `behavior.Fired` now carries `revision_id` (this emission's stable episode-revision handle) and
  `supersedes` (the prior revision it replaces, `None` on an original/provisional fire). A superseding
  emission is metered `<metric>.superseding_revision` — the correction is never silent. Both fields
  default `None`, so a fire with no episode fold is byte-for-byte the legacy shape.
- `incidents.make_incident` carries the link across the emission boundary as a **runtime-local**
  annotation (read via `incident_revision` / `incident_supersedes`) — the frozen wire `Incident` shape,
  its JSON schema, and webhook payloads are untouched (no contract bump).
- `runtime.sinks.SupersessionSink` is the delivery-boundary piece: it resolves the fold's
  revision-level `supersedes` to the **prior incident id** via a bounded `SupersessionLedger`, stamps it
  onto the outgoing incident (`superseded_incident_id`), and **traces every supersession** (structured
  log, or an `on_supersede` callback) — including an unresolved one — so a client debugging the stream
  can always see the original→correction link.
- `slo.SettleWindow` bounds the concern-specific delivery delay: the default is **provisional-immediate**
  (`0.0s`), and a declared window is capped at `2.0s` of **added delivery delay** — how long a client
  waits past a verdict already in hand; a wider window — e.g. one sized to catch a 34s retraction —
  raises rather than silently regress latency. (That cap originally justified itself by reusing a
  "judge round-trip reflex budget" of `2.0s`. That deadline has since been removed — it sat below the
  median provider call time and cancelled the majority of judgments — so the anchor is retired and the
  value held on its own terms. Percept's latency statement is now the measured distribution in
  `percept/slo.py` §5, never a deadline figure.)

Additive and opt-in throughout; `watch.py` / `WATCH_VERSION` and the contract surface are unchanged. A
concern that declares nothing gets today's behaviour.
