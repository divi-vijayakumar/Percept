### Fixed — episodes retain evidence HANDLES, never decoded pixels

`Episode.exemplar` / `Episode.representative` are opaque `Any` and the counting path
(`Percept.count_event`) folded whole DECODED FRAMES into them. Because `max_episodes` bounds the
COUNT of episodes and not their BYTES, a per-episode frame made the log's resident memory scale with
source resolution — the same leak the bounded `FrameWindow` closed — on top of its fixed
`~50 x frame_bytes` plateau (46 MB at 640x480, ~300 MB at 1080p).

- **`percept.episodes.EvidenceHandle`** — the ADR-003 opaque-handle shape: an opaque `ref`, a cheap
  pixel-free `fingerprint` (reusing the perception cache's dHash) the `EpisodeJudge` can compare on,
  and `source_nbytes` (the size of the frame it stands in for, for observability). It carries no
  `nbytes`/`w`/`h`/`size`, so `percept.events.frame_nbytes` — the SAME function the frame-window byte budget
  uses — reads a handle as ~0 retained bytes.
- **`percept.episodes.evidence_handle(exemplar)`** — the single choke point: a decoded frame
  (measured above a small inline threshold) is replaced by a handle and the pixels are never pinned;
  a cheap descriptor (subject token, small dict, pixel-free `Verdict`) passes through byte-for-byte,
  so existing counting behaviour and subject/mutation reads are unchanged.
- **`EpisodeLog`** routes every stored `exemplar`/`representative` (open + continuation) through it
  and exposes **`retained_evidence_nbytes()`** — the sum of `frame_nbytes` over stored evidence. A
  new soak test asserts that over 1500 observations of ~300 KB frames the retained decoded bytes stay
  **flat at zero** and independent of N, bounded by episode COUNT rather than by resolution or by how
  long the process runs. Additive; the gate, defaults, `watch.py` and `WATCH_VERSION` are unchanged.
